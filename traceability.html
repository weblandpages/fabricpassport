FabricPassport / Traceability
Fiber → Fabric → Product

Trace the transformation chain.

A passport becomes more useful when it preserves the relationships between source materials, production events, batches and the final textile product.

A

Fiber source

Cotton, wool, cellulose-based, synthetic or recycled feedstock, including source and supplier references.

B

Yarn production

Spinning event, blend formulation, yarn specification, facility and batch lineage.

C

Fabric formation

Weaving or knitting, construction, weight, width and production batch.

D

Wet processing & finishing

Dyeing, printing, washing, coating, finishing operations and related process evidence.

E

Finished product & lifecycle

Final article association, sale, repair, resale, collection, sorting and recycling events.

Traceability is a graph, not just a list.

Textile supply chains branch and merge. A useful architecture must represent many-to-many relationships between source lots, intermediate batches and final products.

↔

Batch lineage

Record which input lots contributed to each output batch.

⛓

Chain of custody

Preserve evidence about who handled material and under which transformation or transfer event.

◎

Event model

Represent production and logistics as timestamped events linked to identifiers.
